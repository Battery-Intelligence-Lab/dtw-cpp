## Primary-source scope

Keogh and Ratanamahatana, “Exact Indexing of Dynamic Time Warping,”
*Knowledge and Information Systems* 7, 358–386 (2005), DOI
`10.1007/s10115-004-0154-9`, define an upper/lower envelope and prove
LB_Keogh for equal-length series under the same warping window. Their
presentation uses squared point costs and takes a square root after summing.
DTWC++ accumulates either absolute point costs (`U`) or squared point costs
(`U^2`) without a final root. Those repository-specific forms and the
unequal-length prefix extension therefore require the derivation and
independent oracle below; they are not attributed verbatim to the paper.

Lemire, “Streaming Maximum-Minimum Filter Using No More Than Three
Comparisons per Element,” *Nordic Journal of Computing* 13(4), 328–339
(2006), arXiv `cs/0610046`, supplies the monotone-deque filtering technique.
It does not establish DTW lower-bound admissibility.

## Preregistered proof obligations

Let the candidate envelope at row `i` and radius `r` be

```text
L_i = min(candidate_j : |i-j| <= r)
U_i = max(candidate_j : |i-j| <= r).
```

For every query row included in the bound, every admissible path visits at
least one cell `(i,j_i)`. If the DTW radius is at most `r`, then the candidate
value at `j_i` lies in `[L_i,U_i]`. Distance from the query value to that
interval is therefore no larger than the local path-cell cost, for either
absolute or squared point cost. Choosing one distinct path cell per query row
and using non-negative costs proves that the sum of envelope contributions is
no larger than every path cost, hence no larger than DTW. Transposition proves
the reverse direction; the maximum of two lower bounds remains a lower bound.

For unequal lengths, the same row argument applies to any subset of query
rows. In particular, both directional bounds may use the common prefix
`k=min(n,m)`. This is valid only when the envelope radius covers the actual
fixed DTW radius and a path exists (`r >= |n-m|`). It is a
repository-derived extension, not the equal-length proposition in the
primary paper.

Amplitude has arbitrary unit `U`; the radius and indices have unit `sample`.
The L1 bound and DTW have unit `U`; the squared form has unit `U^2`.
There is no approximation in the integer-valued oracle. Floating-point
roundoff at threshold comparisons is outside D2 and remains assigned to D17.
