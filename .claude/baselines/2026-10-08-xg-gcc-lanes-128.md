**Question.** Under GCC on x86-64, with `dtw_lanes<T>` = 128 / sizeof(T) where design-2.0 keeps 64, are the lane
results as bitwise as at 64, does the lanes loop stay free of calls and of spills on the carried values, and is the
fill faster by XL's band?

**Registered band (XL's, per compiler, fixed before any run; the brief verbatim):** "GCC adopts 128 bytes only if every
single-thread lanes shape is ≥ 1.10×, both equal-length fills are ≥ 1.10×, the ragged fill stays within 0.97–1.03×,
and the outputs are as bitwise at 128 as at 64. Otherwise GCC keeps 64 (FALSIFIED for GCC)."

**Status: PASS under Volkan's 10-01 ruling, FALSIFIED under the band's literal last clause** (the reading
fixed before the run; Result below). Found before any timing [confirmed]:

1. **Assembly:** at 128 bytes GCC packs its f64 lanes (ymm `vminpd`, 37 instructions a row step for 16 lanes, no stack
   operand) where at 64 they are scalar (74 instructions for 8 lanes, 16–22 stack operands, carried values among
   them); f32 is packed at both widths (21 → 37 instructions for 16 → 32 lanes); no lanes loop calls anything. XL's
   pre-timing rule passes: the candidate adds no spill on a carried value, it removes base's.
2. **Bitwise:** the L1 lanes are bit for bit at both widths (0 of 8,280,000 outputs differ from the per-pair route; the
   hashes are equal). The squared-L2 lanes are not "as bitwise": packing brings GCC's default contraction
   (`-ffp-contract=fast`) into more of the kernel, so the f64 squared outputs that differ from the per-pair route rise
   from 332 to 673,733 of 4,140,000, and the f32 squared outputs change their own bits too (671,722 → 672,783
   differing); every one within the route bound the tests use (0 outside; worst 0.31–0.33 of its 2).
3. **Clustering:** unchanged. In two WSL trees built with the Linux release flags (base header, candidate header),
   `cpp_conformance` (labels and medoids digit-identical, scores within 1e-12, against the tracked reference) and the
   lanes tests pass, and the regenerated conformance reference equals the tracked one in both. 64 runs of the real CLI
   per build: labels and medoids identical in 64/64. The largest relative difference is 1.26e-16 for a total cost and
   6.3e-16 for a squared distance-matrix entry; L1 matrices are identical.

**The two readings of the band's last criterion.** Literal, "the outputs are as bitwise at 128 as at 64": every lane
output equal to base's. Squared L2 fails that whatever the timing, so GCC would keep 64 (FALSIFIED). Under Volkan's
10-01 ruling (DECISIONS: keep FMA contraction; last-bit differences between routes and compilers are fine; clustering
results must not change), which the orchestrator chose before the run (2026-10-08): the L1 lanes bit for bit, the
squared lanes within the route bound, the clustering identical. All three hold [confirmed], so under the ruling GCC is
judged on the timing band alone. The kit's report prints the verdict under the ruling and the literal one beside it.

## The candidate

## Assembly [confirmed]

The LTO-linked probes, `objdump -d -M intel` (`asm/gcc_<v>_xprobe.dis`; the code that runs; GCC inlines the kernel into
the block function's `std::function` invoker, or for f32 at 128 bytes into the lambda's call operator), read by XL's
`tools/loops.py`; the L1 loops in `asm/gcc_<v>_l1.txt`. The non-LTO `-S -masm=intel` listing of `dtw_lanes.cpp`
(`asm/gcc_<v>_dtw_lanes.s`) agrees: candidate and f32 loops identical in counts, base f64 73 and 74 instructions with
20 and 16 stack operands, no call (`asm/loops_summary.txt`). One DP row step = one `i` of the kernel's inner loop. GCC
keeps two copies of the row loop where the table says so; both are listed.

| loop (L1) | base (W 8 doubles / 16 floats) | candidate (W 16 / 32) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| f64 | scalar `vminsd`, 16 a row step; 74 instructions (9.25 a cell); 22 and 16 rsp operands in the two copies: 8 reloads of the loop-invariant y lanes, the rest stores and reloads of carried `diag`/`left` values (and one cost temporary) | packed ymm `vminpd`, 8 a row step; 37 instructions (2.31 a cell); no stack operand; both copies |
| f32 | packed ymm `vminps`, 4 a row step; 21 instructions (1.31 a cell); no stack operand; both copies | packed ymm `vminps`, 8 a row step; 37 instructions (1.16 a cell); no stack operand; one copy |

No lanes loop of either build calls anything. Squared L2: in the row loop (`asm/gcc_sq_arith.txt`) base f64 runs
`vmulsd` 8 + `vaddsd` 8 a row step, the candidate `vfmadd231pd`/`vfmadd132pd` 4; f32 `vfmadd231ps` at both widths (2 /
4). Over each whole lanes function (`asm/gcc_fma_counts.txt`, less the row loops' share) the candidate also packs and
contracts parts of the kernel outside the row loop: FMA instructions there f64 squared 1 → 5, f32 squared 3 → 8
(which part each sits in is not traced). The per-pair univariate squared kernel (`detail::dtw_linear<…SpanSquaredL2Cost>`) is `vmulsd`/`vmulss`,
unfused, in both builds. Why GCC fuses the packed forms and not the scalar ones: not investigated. The probe's edit for
the route bound (below) left every lanes loop's counts unchanged (`asm/loops_summary.txt` before and after: identical).
